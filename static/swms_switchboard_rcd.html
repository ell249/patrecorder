Testing of Fixed RCDs at Switchboards

1. Scope and Purpose

This Safe Work Method Statement (SWMS) describes the safe work procedures for push-button and trip-time testing of fixed Residual Current Devices (RCDs) installed at distribution switchboards, in accordance with:

  • AS/NZS 3760:2022 Appendix B – Testing of fixed RCDs (as distinct from portable, in-line RCDs tested with a lead).
  • AS/NZS 3000:2018 (Wiring Rules) – Referenced for switchboard access, enclosure, and RCD installation requirements.

This SWMS applies only to testing a fixed RCD already installed and commissioned at a switchboard (push-button function test and instrument trip-time test). It does not cover installation, replacement, wiring, or any other work inside a switchboard enclosure that involves exposure to internal live parts beyond operating the RCD's own test button and connecting an external RCD tester to an adjacent socket outlet or test point. Any such additional work is outside this SWMS and requires a separate risk assessment and an appropriately licensed electrical worker.

Key distinction from portable appliance (PAT) testing: this work takes place at a live distribution switchboard rather than on an isolated, unplugged appliance. The hazards, competency requirements, and controls in this document are specific to that environment and are more stringent than those in the general PAT Testing SWMS.

3. Personnel Competency and Authorisation

Testing of fixed RCDs at a switchboard must only be performed by a person who is both:

  • A competent person as defined by AS/NZS 3760:2022 (test and tag knowledge, calibrated instrument operation, applicable standards), and
  • Where the switchboard enclosure must be opened, or the test point is not externally accessible, a person holding a current electrical licence valid in the relevant state or territory, or working under the direct supervision of one.

A person carrying out this work must also:

  • Be familiar with this SWMS and the specific hazards of the switchboard and site.
  • Hold a valid RCD/PAT testing and tagging certificate from a registered training organisation (RTO) where applicable.
  • Have received site-specific induction covering switchboard locations, isolation procedures, and emergency contacts.

Persons who are not licensed for restricted electrical work must not open switchboard enclosures or access internal live parts. If in doubt about whether a given switchboard's test point is externally accessible, treat it as requiring a licensed electrician.

6. Pre-Task Requirements

  • Obtain authorisation from the site manager / PCBU and, where applicable, an electrical work permit before opening any switchboard.
  • Confirm the tester's licence/competency is appropriate for the specific switchboard access required (see Section 3).
  • Conduct a site-specific hazard assessment, including checking for any existing arc flash study or electrical safety management plan for the switchboard.
  • Ensure all test equipment calibration certificates are current.
  • Notify affected building occupants or workers that an RCD test may cause a momentary loss of power to circuits protected by that RCD, and schedule accordingly to minimise disruption (e.g. outside production hours, or with critical loads confirmed unaffected).
  • Identify the specific RCD(s) to be tested against the switchboard's circuit schedule before starting, and confirm this matches the Switchboard record in PAT Recorder.
  • Verify the work area in front of the switchboard is dry, clear, and has adequate lighting and access/egress space per AS/NZS 3000 clearance requirements.
  • Ensure an emergency plan and first aid kit are accessible, and that the location of the main switchboard isolator is known.

8. Step-by-Step Work Procedure

Step 1 — Site Induction and Setup

  1. Sign in at site reception or notify the site manager of your presence and intended work area.
  2. Complete any required site-specific induction and obtain any electrical work permit required by the site.
  3. Locate the switchboard(s) to be tested and confirm against the Switchboard record in PAT Recorder.
  4. Inspect the work area for hazards (wet floors, overhead hazards, confined space, traffic). Apply controls before proceeding.
  5. Put on required PPE for the switchboard's arc flash risk category.

Step 2 — RCD Identification

  1. Identify the fixed RCD by its switchboard, circuit label, and asset record. Record/confirm in PAT Recorder against the correct Switchboard.
  2. Note the RCD Type (Type I / Type II) and Rating (mA) from the device label — this determines the applicable trip-time limit (≤ 40 ms for Type I, 10 mA; ≤ 300 ms for Type II, 30 mA).
  3. Confirm whether a push-button test, a trip-time test, or both are required for this visit, per the schedule in Section 9.

Step 3 — Push-Button Test

  1. Where the RCD's test button is externally accessible without opening the switchboard enclosure, this step may be performed by a competent person without an electrical licence.
  2. Advise affected areas that a momentary loss of power may occur to circuits protected by this RCD.
  3. Press the RCD's integral test button firmly. The RCD should trip immediately, disconnecting the protected circuit(s).
  4. PASS criterion: the RCD trips without undue delay when the test button is pressed. If it does not trip, record FAIL and quarantine/report the circuit — do not rely on this RCD for protection until repaired.
  5. Reset the RCD to restore supply once the result is recorded.
  6. Record the result (PASS/FAIL) against the RCD's test record in PAT Recorder.

Step 4 — Trip-Time Test (Competent Person, Instrument Test)

  1. Confirm the switchboard's test point (a dedicated test socket, or terminals accessible per Section 3) is suitable for connecting the RCD tester without exposing live parts beyond the test point itself.
  2. If the enclosure must be opened to access the test point, this step must only be performed by, or under the direct supervision of, a licensed electrical worker (see Section 3), following isolation/proving procedures for any other live parts nearby.
  3. Connect the calibrated RCD tester to the test point in accordance with the tester manufacturer's instructions.
  4. Select the correct test parameters for the RCD's Type and Rating on the tester.
  5. Record trip time at the 0° plug/connection orientation, then repeat at the 180° orientation (reversing the test connection), as some devices are sensitive to connection polarity.
  6. PASS criterion at each orientation: trip time ≤ 40 ms for Type I (10 mA) or ≤ 300 ms for Type II (30 mA). A FAIL at either orientation is an overall FAIL for the RCD.
  7. Disconnect the tester, close and secure the switchboard enclosure, and reset the RCD to restore supply.
  8. Record both trip-time results and the computed PASS/FAIL against the RCD's test record in PAT Recorder.

Step 5 — Overall Assessment and Reporting

  1. Determine the overall result (PASS or FAIL) for the RCD based on the push-button and/or trip-time results recorded.
  2. If FAIL:
    • Advise the site manager in writing immediately — a failed RCD is a safety-critical finding, as it may no longer provide protection against electric shock or fire.
    • Do not rely on the RCD for protection until it has been repaired or replaced and re-tested by a licensed electrician.
    • Consider whether affected circuits should remain in service pending rectification, in consultation with the site manager/PCBU.
  3. Record all results in PAT Recorder against the correct Switchboard and RCD, including tester name, certificate/licence number, date, RCD Type/Rating, and next test due date.
  4. Generate the Switchboard RCD Report from PAT Recorder if a consolidated compliance record for the switchboard is required by the site.

Step 6 — Pack Up and Site Handover

  1. Confirm all switchboard enclosures opened during the visit are closed and secured.
  2. Confirm all RCDs tested have been reset and supply restored to affected circuits.
  3. Remove all test equipment, signage, and barrier tape from the work area.
  4. Advise the site manager that testing is complete and provide a verbal summary of any failed RCDs.
  5. Ensure the electronic test register (PAT Recorder) is saved and backed up before leaving site.
  6. Sign out at site reception if required.

10. Emergency Procedures

Electric Shock

  1. Do not touch the person if they are still in contact with the electrical source.
  2. Isolate the power at the switchboard's main isolator or the nearest switch/circuit breaker.
  3. Once safe, call 000 immediately.
  4. Commence CPR if the person is unresponsive and not breathing. Use the AED if available.
  5. Keep the person warm and still until emergency services arrive.
  6. Do not allow the person to return to work — all electric shock incidents must be assessed by a medical professional.

Arc Flash / Fire

  1. Move away from the switchboard immediately and activate the nearest fire alarm.
  2. Call 000.
  3. Evacuate the area using the designated emergency exits.
  4. Only use a fire extinguisher (CO₂ or dry chemical for electrical fires) if the fire is small, you are trained to do so, and you have a clear exit behind you.
  5. Assemble at the designated muster point and report to the fire warden.

Injury

  1. For serious injury, call 000 and do not move the injured person unless there is immediate danger.
  2. For minor injury, use the first aid kit and advise the site first aid officer.
  3. All incidents and near misses must be reported to the site manager and recorded in the incident register.
  4. Notify your employer of any notifiable incidents per WHS Act s. 35–38.

11. Incident Reporting and Review

All incidents, near misses, and hazards identified during testing must be reported to the site manager and your employer. Notifiable incidents (death, serious injury, or dangerous incident) must be reported to the relevant state/territory WHS regulator immediately — do not disturb the scene except to assist injured persons or make the area safe.

This SWMS must be reviewed after any incident and at minimum annually. Amendments must be authorised and all personnel re-briefed.
