SAFE WORK METHOD STATEMENT

Repair and Maintenance of Portable Electrical Appliances

1. Scope and Purpose

This Safe Work Method Statement (SWMS) describes safe work procedures for the repair and maintenance of portable electrical appliances in workplace, commercial, and industrial environments. It covers work such as:

  • Replacement of supply cords, plugs, and cord connectors
  • Replacement of fuses, switches, or internal components
  • Repair or replacement of heating elements, motors, or controls
  • Internal fault diagnosis and rectification
  • Any repair activity requiring the appliance to be opened, disassembled, or have its wiring disturbed

Following any repair covered by this SWMS, the appliance must be tested and tagged by a competent person in accordance with AS/NZS 5762:2011 (In-service safety inspection and testing of electrical equipment after repair) before being returned to service.

InstrumentRelevance
Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (Cth/State) Imposes a primary duty of care on the PCBU (s. 17–19) to ensure work is performed without risk to health and safety. Workers also have duties under s. 28 to take reasonable care for their own safety and that of others.

State/Territory Electrical Safety Acts Certain repair work constitutes electrical work that must only be performed by a licensed electrician under state and territory legislation (e.g., Electrical Safety Act 2002 (Qld), Electricity Safety Act 1998 (Vic), Electrical Equipment Safety System (national)). Verify whether the repair task requires a licensed electrician in your jurisdiction before proceeding.
AS/NZS 5762:2011 Specifies inspection and test requirements for portable electrical equipment after repair. All repairs must be followed by testing to this standard before the equipment is returned to service.
AS/NZS 3760:2022 Provides the base in-service inspection and test framework that AS/NZS 5762 builds upon; defines pass/fail criteria, tagging requirements, and retest intervals.
AS/NZS 3000:2018 (Wiring Rules) Governs the correct wiring methods, conductor colours, termination standards, and earthing requirements that must be observed in any repair involving internal wiring.
AS/NZS 3820:1998 Principles of electrical safety — relevant to risk assessment when designing and executing repair procedures.

3. Licensing, Competency, and Authorisation

Important — Electrical Licensing: In Australia, internal wiring repairs to an appliance (including replacing a plug or cord where the supply cord is hardwired) may constitute electrical work and must only be performed by a licensed electrician or holder of the relevant restricted electrical licence. Check your state or territory regulations before assigning repair tasks to unlicensed personnel. Penalties for unlicensed electrical work can be significant.

A person performing repairs under this SWMS must:

  • Hold the required licence or trade qualification for the type of work being performed (e.g., electrical licence, appliance serviceman's licence, or restricted electrical worker authorisation as applicable).
  • Be competent in the specific repair task — having the knowledge, skills, and experience relevant to the appliance type and fault being rectified.
  • Be familiar with this SWMS and the specific hazards of the work area.
  • Ensure a qualified person (competent under AS/NZS 5762) tests the appliance after repair — this may be the same person if they hold both the repair qualification and PAT testing competency.

6. Pre-Task Requirements

  • Confirm the repair task is within the scope of your licence, qualifications, and competency. If not, escalate to an appropriately qualified tradesperson.
  • Obtain authorisation from the site manager / PCBU before commencing work.
  • Conduct a site-specific hazard assessment and review any site safety inductions required.
  • Inspect all tools for damaged insulation or defects before use. Remove any defective tools from service.
  • Identify and confirm the location of the nearest electrical isolation point (switchboard, power outlet, circuit breaker) for the appliance being repaired.
  • Ensure the work area is dry, well-lit, and has adequate ventilation for soldering or solvent use.
  • Confirm a first aid kit and emergency plan are accessible before commencing work.
  • Brief other workers in the area that repair work will be taking place.

  • Isolate appliance and allow adequate discharge time per manufacturer specification before opening.
  • Use an approved capacitor discharge tool to verify and safely discharge capacitors before touching internal components.
  • Never short capacitor terminals with an uninsulated screwdriver or wire — use a discharge resistor rated for the energy stored.
  • Wear insulating gloves and safety glasses whenever working near capacitors.

3 Arc flash from inadvertent short-circuit during live diagnostic work Repairer High
  • De-energise before all internal access. Only a licensed electrician may work on live circuits, and only when de-energised work is not reasonably practicable.
  • Where live work is unavoidable: use insulated probes; cover adjacent live parts with insulating mats; keep test leads clear of terminals not being measured.
  • Wear face shield and insulating gloves for any live diagnostic work.
 Medium
4 Cuts and lacerations from sheet metal edges, broken components, or tools Repairer Medium
  • Inspect enclosure and components for sharp edges before reaching inside.
  • Use insulated pliers rather than bare hands to handle internal components.
  • Wear cut-resistant gloves where handling sheet metal panels (confirm gloves are compatible with the electrical hazard level present).
 Low
5 Burns from soldering iron, hot components, or heating elements Repairer Medium
  • Use a temperature-controlled soldering station with a stable holder — never lay the iron flat.
  • Allow sufficient cooling time for components and heating elements before handling.
  • Keep flammable materials away from the soldering area.
  • Wear safety glasses to protect against solder splash.
 Low
6 Inhalation of flux fumes, solder vapour, or cleaning solvents Repairer Medium
  • Solder in a well-ventilated area or use a fume extractor positioned close to the work.
  • Use lead-free, low-flux-activity solder where possible.
  • Wear a P2 respirator when soldering in confined or poorly ventilated spaces, or when using cleaning solvents.
  • Review Safety Data Sheets (SDS) for all chemicals used.
 Low
7 Fire from incorrect reassembly, incorrect replacement parts, or omitting insulating materials Repairer, end users, building occupants High
  • Eliminate: Use only manufacturer-approved or equivalent rated replacement parts. Never substitute with lower-rated components.
  • Reinstate all original thermal insulation, cable ties, and clamping arrangements before reassembly.
  • Perform a post-repair visual inspection confirming no conductors are trapped, pinched, or contacting heating elements.
  • Complete post-repair testing per AS/NZS 5762 — a fire hazard from incorrect wiring will often present as a failed insulation resistance or leakage test.
 Low
8 Incorrect wiring causing shock risk or equipment damage to end user after repair End users, bystanders High
  • Photograph the internal wiring arrangement before disassembly.
  • Label all conductors removed during disassembly.
  • Verify conductor colours against AS/NZS 3000:2018 (brown=active, blue=neutral, green/yellow=earth).
  • Conduct a polarity check during post-repair PAT testing to confirm active, neutral, and earth are correctly connected.
 Low
9 Manual handling injury when moving heavy appliances Repairer Medium
  • Repair appliances in situ on a workbench where possible.
  • Use team lifts or trolleys for items exceeding 16 kg.
  • Maintain neutral spine posture; do not twist when lifting.
 Low
10 Slip, trip or fall — tools, parts, or leads on floor Repairer, bystanders Medium
  • Maintain a tidy work area — return tools to the kit after each use.
  • Store removed components in a tray or container to prevent items rolling onto the floor.
  • Route extension leads and test leads around the perimeter of the work area.
 Low

8. Step-by-Step Work Procedure

Step 1 — Pre-Repair Assessment

  1. Confirm your qualifications and licence authorise you to perform the specific repair. If not, stop and escalate.
  2. Identify the appliance by asset number, description, and location. Record the repair details in PAT Recorder.
  3. Review the fault description and any previous repair or test history for the appliance.
  4. Photograph the appliance in its current state (damage, fault indicators, wiring layout) before disassembly.
  5. Assess the work area for hazards specific to this task and apply controls from Section 7.

Step 2 — Isolation and Lock-Out / Tag-Out

  1. Disconnect the appliance from the power supply. Do not rely on the appliance's own power switch — physically remove the plug from the outlet.
  2. If the appliance is hardwired or cannot be unplugged, isolate at the circuit breaker or isolating switch and apply a padlock and DANGER tag (lock-out / tag-out). Only the person performing the repair should hold the key.
  3. Verify isolation using a voltage tester (non-contact preferred) on the supply terminals before touching any internal parts.
  4. If the appliance contains large capacitors (e.g., microwave oven, motor drive), discharge them using an approved discharge tool and verify with a multimeter before proceeding.

Step 3 — Fault Diagnosis (De-Energised)

  1. Open the appliance enclosure using insulated tools. Handle sharp edges with care.
  2. Inspect internal components visually: look for burn marks, corrosion, cracked insulation, loose terminals, or obvious failed components.
  3. Using a multimeter (de-energised), perform continuity and resistance checks to confirm the fault.
  4. If energised testing is required to diagnose the fault, this must be performed only by a licensed electrician following an approved live-work procedure with appropriate PPE. All other personnel must stand clear.
  5. Document the fault and proposed repair in PAT Recorder before proceeding.

Step 4 — Repair Execution

  1. Source approved replacement parts of equivalent or greater specification to the original.
  2. Ensure isolation remains in place throughout the repair.
  3. Replace faulty components using appropriate tools and connection methods (crimp, screw terminal, or soldering as applicable to the circuit).
  4. When replacing supply cord or plug:
    • Use flex of equivalent rating to the original (conductor cross-section, insulation rating, earthing configuration).
    • Observe correct conductor colour coding: Brown = Active (L), Blue = Neutral (N), Green/Yellow = Earth (E) per AS/NZS 3000.
    • Ensure all terminations are mechanically secure — no loose strands, no exposed conductors beyond the terminal.
    • Reinstate cord grip/strain relief securely so that tension on the cord is not transferred to the terminal connections.
  5. Reinstate all original insulating materials, cable retaining clips, and cord clamps removed during disassembly.
  6. Verify no conductors are trapped, pinched, or in contact with heating elements or moving parts before closing the enclosure.
  7. Close and secure the enclosure fully — all screws replaced, covers fitted, guards reinstated.

Step 5 — Post-Repair Visual Inspection

  1. Before applying power, conduct a thorough visual inspection of the completed repair:
    • Plug — correct assembly, no exposed conductors, strain relief secure.
    • Supply cord — no damage introduced during repair, not pinched by enclosure.
    • Enclosure — all covers and guards in place, no missing screws or fasteners.
    • Internal wiring — all terminals correct, conductors correctly identified, no loose strands.
  2. Record any observations in PAT Recorder under the repair comments field.

Step 6 — Post-Repair Testing (AS/NZS 5762:2011)

All repaired appliances must be tested by a competent person before being returned to service. Testing must meet the requirements of AS/NZS 5762:2011, which incorporates the inspection and test procedures of AS/NZS 3760. The following tests must be performed and recorded:

  1. Earth continuity (Class I appliances): ≤ 1.0 Ω including test lead resistance.
  2. Insulation resistance: ≥ 1 MΩ at 500 V DC.
  3. Leakage current (if applicable): ≤ 5 mA (Class I) or ≤ 1 mA (Class II).
  4. Polarity test: confirm active, neutral, and earth are correctly wired.
  5. Functional test: operate the appliance under normal conditions and confirm correct operation following repair.

Record all test results in PAT Recorder. A failed test result means the appliance must not be returned to service.

Step 7 — Tagging and Return to Service

  1. If post-repair testing PASSES:
    • Apply a new colour-coded PASS tag per AS/NZS 3760 Appendix C indicating the test date and next test due date.
    • Remove any previous FAIL or DANGER tags.
    • Record the completed repair and test in PAT Recorder, including tester name, certificate number, all electrical measurements, and the post-repair functional test result.
    • Return the appliance to service and advise the site manager.
  2. If post-repair testing FAILS:
    • Apply a red DANGER / OUT OF SERVICE tag.
    • Do not return the appliance to service. Advise the site manager in writing.
    • Determine whether further repair is viable or the appliance should be disposed of.

Step 8 — Pack Up and Documentation

  1. Remove all tools, replaced components, and waste materials from the work area. Dispose of waste per site procedures.
  2. Remove the lock-out/tag-out device only after the repair is fully complete and verified safe.
  3. Confirm all repair and test data is saved in PAT Recorder before leaving site.
  4. Advise the site manager of the outcome, any quarantined equipment, and any follow-up actions required.

9. Emergency Procedures

Electric Shock

  1. Do not touch the person if they are still in contact with the electrical source.
  2. Isolate the power at the nearest switch, circuit breaker, or by removing the plug — whichever is fastest and safest.
  3. Call 000 immediately.
  4. Once safe, commence CPR if the person is unresponsive and not breathing. Use the AED if available.
  5. Keep the person still and warm until emergency services arrive. All electric shock incidents require medical assessment even if the person appears uninjured.

Fire

  1. Activate the nearest fire alarm.
  2. Call 000.
  3. Isolate electrical power if safe to do so.
  4. Evacuate using designated exits.
  5. Only use a CO₂ or dry chemical extinguisher on an electrical fire if you are trained to do so and have a clear exit behind you.
  6. Assemble at the designated muster point and report to the fire warden.

Injury

  1. For serious injury, call 000 and do not move the person unless in immediate danger.
  2. For minor injury, use the first aid kit and advise the site first aid officer.
  3. All incidents and near misses must be reported to the site manager and recorded in the incident register.
  4. Notify your employer of notifiable incidents per WHS Act ss. 35–38.

10. Incident Reporting and Review

All incidents, near misses, and hazards encountered during repair work must be reported to the site manager and your employer. Notifiable incidents (death, serious injury, or dangerous incident) must be reported immediately to the relevant state/territory WHS regulator — do not disturb the scene except to assist injured persons or make the area safe.

This SWMS must be reviewed after any incident, after any change to the repair tasks or work environment, and at a minimum annually. All amendments must be authorised and workers re-briefed.
