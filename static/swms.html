SAFE WORK METHOD STATEMENT

In-Service Electrical Safety Testing of Portable Appliances

1. Scope and Purpose

This Safe Work Method Statement (SWMS) describes the safe work procedures for conducting in-service safety inspection and testing of portable electrical appliances in accordance with:

  • AS/NZS 3760:2022 – In-service safety inspection and testing of electrical equipment
  • AS/NZS 5761:2011 – In-service safety inspection and testing of second-hand electrical equipment prior to sale
  • AS/NZS 5762:2011 – In-service safety inspection and testing of electrical equipment after repair

This SWMS applies to all testing activities carried out by competent persons in workplace, commercial, industrial, and residential environments covered by the above standards.

3. Personnel Competency and Authorisation

Testing must only be performed by a competent person as defined by AS/NZS 3760:2022. A competent person:

  • Has knowledge and skills appropriate to the nature of the work (electrical theory, test equipment operation, applicable standards).
  • Holds current relevant qualifications (e.g., Certificate IV in Electrical Instrumentation, or equivalent trade qualification recognised by the relevant state authority).
  • Holds a valid testing and tagging certificate from a registered training organisation (RTO) accredited to deliver the applicable units of competency.
  • Is familiar with this SWMS and the specific hazards of the site.

Persons who are not competent must not perform PAT testing.

6. Pre-Task Requirements

  • Obtain authorisation from the site manager / PCBU to conduct testing in the work area.
  • Conduct a site-specific hazard assessment and review any site safety inductions required.
  • Ensure all test equipment calibration certificates are current.
  • Brief all workers in the area that electrical testing will be taking place and may require temporary disconnection of equipment.
  • Identify and segregate any equipment that is energised and cannot be de-energised. Do not test energised equipment.
  • Verify that the work area is dry. Do not conduct testing in wet conditions or near water without additional controls.
  • Ensure an emergency plan and first aid kit are accessible.
  • Complete all relevant site permits to work (hot work, electrical isolation) if required by site rules.

8. Step-by-Step Work Procedure

Step 1 — Site Induction and Setup

  1. Sign in at site reception or notify the site manager of your presence and intended work area.
  2. Complete any required site-specific induction.
  3. Inspect the work area for hazards (wet floors, overhead hazards, traffic). Apply controls before proceeding.
  4. Set up test equipment on a stable, dry surface. Confirm calibration is current.
  5. Put on required PPE.

Step 2 — Equipment Identification

  1. Identify the appliance by asset number, description, and location. Record in PAT Recorder.
  2. Confirm the applicable test standard (AS/NZS 3760, 5761, or 5762) based on the nature of the equipment and reason for testing.
  3. Note the appliance class type (Class I — earthed; Class II — double insulated) as this determines which tests apply.

Step 3 — Disconnect and Visual Inspection

  1. Disconnect the appliance from the mains supply before inspection.
  2. Inspect the plug: check for cracks, burns, missing insulation, or damaged pins. Record PASS/FAIL.
  3. Inspect the supply cord along its full length: look for cuts, abrasions, kinks, exposed conductors, or taped repairs. Record PASS/FAIL.
  4. Inspect the appliance casing/enclosure: check for cracks, missing covers, loose screws, or damaged guards. Record PASS/FAIL/N/A as appropriate.
  5. Check for signs of overheating: discolouration, scorch marks, deformed plastic, or burning odour. Record PASS/FAIL.
  6. Check the rating label: confirm it is present, legible, and that the rated voltage/frequency matches site supply. Record PASS/FAIL/N/A as appropriate.
  7. Check for exposed conductors — no live, neutral, or earth conductors should be accessible. Record PASS/FAIL.
  8. Check for evidence of unsafe previous repairs (taped joints, non-approved connectors). Record PASS/FAIL/N/A.
  9. Verify cable strain relief / cord grip is correctly fitted. Record PASS/FAIL/N/A.
  10. Verify safety functions and guards operate correctly. Record PASS/FAIL/N/A.
  11. If any visual inspection item FAILS, quarantine the appliance immediately using a red DANGER tag. Do not proceed to electrical testing — a failed visual inspection is an automatic overall FAIL.

Step 4 — Electrical Testing

Only conduct electrical testing if the visual inspection has passed in all required areas.

  1. Earth Continuity (Class I appliances only):
    Connect the PAT tester to the appliance plug and the accessible metal parts. Measure earth path resistance using DC test current per AS/NZS 3760 Table 2.
    PASS criterion: ≤ 1.0 Ω (including test lead resistance — subtract lead resistance from reading).
    Class II appliances: record as N/A.
  2. Insulation Resistance:
    Apply 500 V DC between live conductors (Active + Neutral bonded together) and the appliance earth or accessible metal.
    PASS criterion: ≥ 1 MΩ per AS/NZS 3760 Table 3.
    Ensure the appliance is switched ON during the insulation test so that the internal wiring is included.
  3. Leakage Current (if applicable):
    Conduct a substitute/touch current test where required by the test type.
    PASS criterion: ≤ 5 mA (Class I) or ≤ 1 mA (Class II) per AS/NZS 3760 Table 4.
  4. Polarity Test (if applicable):
    Confirm Active, Neutral, and Earth are correctly wired using the PAT tester polarity function or a dedicated polarity tester.
    PASS: all conductors correctly connected.
  5. RCD Test (Lead+RCD test type):
    Test RCD using a dedicated RCD tester at rated trip current.
    PASS criterion: trip time ≤ 300 ms (Type II, 30 mA) or ≤ 40 ms (Type I, 10 mA).

Step 5 — Additional Requirements by Standard

AS/NZS 5761 (Second-hand equipment)

  • Complete a condition assessment: document the physical condition beyond the VI checklist.
  • Perform a functional check: operate the appliance under normal conditions and confirm it functions as intended.
  • List any accessories included.
  • Record a safe-for-resale determination (YES or NO).

AS/NZS 5762 (Equipment after repair)

  • Document the repair description in detail — what was replaced or repaired.
  • Record the name of the repairer (Repaired By).
  • List all parts replaced.
  • Conduct and document a post-repair functional test: operate the appliance and confirm correct operation after repair.

Step 6 — Overall Assessment and Tagging

  1. Determine the overall result (PASS or FAIL) based on all visual inspection and electrical test results.
  2. If PASS:
    • Apply a colour-coded PASS tag in accordance with AS/NZS 3760 Appendix C (colour corresponds to the retest period).
    • Tag must include: tester name, test date, next test due date, and test standard.
  3. If FAIL:
    • Apply a red DANGER / OUT OF SERVICE tag clearly identifying the equipment as unsafe.
    • Remove the equipment from service immediately. Advise the site manager in writing.
    • Do not return failed equipment to service until it has been repaired and re-tested.
  4. Record all test results in PAT Recorder, including tester name, certificate number, date, and all individual measurements.

Step 7 — Pack Up and Site Handover

  1. Remove all test equipment, leads, and personal equipment from the work area.
  2. Restore any furniture or equipment moved during testing.
  3. Advise the site manager that testing is complete and provide a verbal summary of any failed or quarantined equipment.
  4. Ensure the electronic test register (PAT Recorder) is saved and backed up before leaving site.
  5. Sign out at site reception if required.

9. Emergency Procedures

Electric Shock

  1. Do not touch the person if they are still in contact with the electrical source.
  2. Isolate the power at the nearest switch or circuit breaker.
  3. Once safe, call 000 immediately.
  4. Commence CPR if the person is unresponsive and not breathing. Use the AED if available.
  5. Keep the person warm and still until emergency services arrive.
  6. Do not allow the person to return to work — all electric shock incidents must be assessed by a medical professional.

Fire

  1. Activate the nearest fire alarm.
  2. Call 000.
  3. Evacuate the area using the designated emergency exits.
  4. Only use a fire extinguisher (CO₂ or dry chemical for electrical fires) if the fire is small, you are trained to do so, and you have a clear exit behind you.
  5. Assemble at the designated muster point and report to the fire warden.

Injury

  1. For serious injury, call 000 and do not move the injured person unless there is immediate danger.
  2. For minor injury, use the first aid kit and advise the site first aid officer.
  3. All incidents and near misses must be reported to the site manager and recorded in the incident register.
  4. Notify your employer of any notifiable incidents per WHS Act s. 35–38.

10. Incident Reporting and Review

All incidents, near misses, and hazards identified during testing must be reported to the site manager and your employer. Notifiable incidents (death, serious injury, or dangerous incident) must be reported to the relevant state/territory WHS regulator immediately — do not disturb the scene except to assist injured persons or make the area safe.

This SWMS must be reviewed after any incident and at minimum annually. Amendments must be authorised and all personnel re-briefed.
